Ready-to-use prompts ✨

Copy, fill in the [brackets], and paste into any capable AI assistant or agent (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and others). Each one follows the recipe from Giving great instructions.

🃏 Turn my notes into flashcards

After a lecture or reading, when you want to review with active recall instead of rereading.

I'm studying [subject] for [exam or goal, e.g. my biology midterm on 12 May]. I want to review with active recall, not by rereading.
Here are my notes: [paste notes].

Please turn them into 20 flashcards.
- One idea per card. The question side should make me retrieve the answer, not just recognise it.
- Mix question types: definitions, "why" questions, and "what would happen if…" questions.
- Keep each answer under 25 words.
- Only use facts from my notes. If something in my notes looks wrong or unclear, list it separately at the end.

Format: a table with columns Question | Answer, so I can paste it into a flashcard app.
🎯 Quiz me, one question at a time

When you want to test yourself on a topic and find your weak spots before an exam.

Act as my quizmaster for [topic]. My level: [beginner / intermediate / advanced]. My goal: [e.g. pass the driving theory test].

Rules:
- Ask me ONE question at a time and wait for my answer.
- Start easy and get harder when I get two in a row right; go easier if I get one wrong.
- After each answer, tell me if I'm right, explain briefly why, and correct any misunderstanding.
- Don't give me the answer before I've tried.

After 10 questions, stop and give me: my score, the 2–3 ideas I struggled with most, and what to review next.
🦉 Be my Socratic tutor

When you want to truly understand something by working it out yourself, not just be told the answer.

I want to understand [concept or problem, e.g. why compound interest grows so fast]. Please tutor me using the Socratic method.

- Don't give me the answer. Ask me one guiding question at a time.
- Start by asking what I already know, so you can build on it.
- If I'm stuck twice in a row, give me a small hint, not the solution.
- If I say something wrong, ask a question that helps me notice the mistake myself.
- When I've got it, ask me to explain the whole idea back in my own words, and tell me if anything is missing.
🧒 Explain it like I'm new to this

When a topic feels full of jargon and you need a clear, friendly first explanation.

Explain [topic, e.g. how inflation works] to me. I'm completely new to it, and I'm [who you are, e.g. a first-year nursing student / a manager with no finance background].

Please:
1. Start with an everyday analogy.
2. Then give a plain explanation in under 200 words. Define every technical term the first time you use it.
3. Give one real-life example.
4. List 3 common misconceptions and why they're wrong.
5. End with 3 quick questions I can answer to check I understood (answers at the very bottom).
🗓️ Build me a realistic study plan

When you have an exam or deadline coming and need a plan you can actually stick to.

Help me make a study plan. My goal: [e.g. pass my statistics exam]. The exam is on [date]. Today is [date].
Topics to cover: [list topics, and mark the ones you find hardest].
Time I really have: [e.g. 1 hour on weekdays, 3 hours on Saturdays, nothing on Sundays].

Requirements:
- Use spaced repetition: revisit each topic at least 3 times, with growing gaps.
- Put the hardest topics earlier, and include practice tests in the last week.
- Leave one buffer day per week for catching up.
- Keep each session to a single, specific task (e.g. "Do 10 practice problems on probability", not "Study probability").

Format: a table by date with columns Day | Session task | Minutes. Then 3 tips for staying on track.
🧑‍🏫 Check my understanding with the Feynman technique

When you think you understand something and want to find the gaps.

I'm going to explain [topic] in my own words, as if teaching a 12-year-old. Please act as a curious student and a strict but kind teacher.

My explanation: [write your explanation here].

Then:
1. Point out anything that's wrong, vague, or skipped.
2. Ask me 2 "but why?" questions that poke at the weakest part of my explanation.
3. Tell me which part I explained well.
Don't rewrite the whole thing for me — help me fix it myself.
🌱 Learn a new skill in 30 days

When you want to pick up a new skill (a language, Excel, drawing) and don't know where to start.

I want to learn [skill] so that I can [specific goal, e.g. hold a 5-minute conversation in Spanish on holiday]. I can spend [minutes] a day for 30 days. My starting point: [what you can do now].

Design a 30-day plan:
- Focus on the 20% of the skill that gives 80% of the results for my goal.
- Each day: one small practice task with a clear "done" signal.
- Every 7 days: a mini-challenge to test my progress.
- Suggest free or low-cost resources by type (e.g. "a beginner podcast"), and don't invent specific titles if you're not sure they exist.

Format: weeks as headings, days as a numbered list.
✉️ Draft an email that gets a reply

When you need to write an important email and want it clear, short and polite.

Help me write an email to [who, and your relationship, e.g. my landlord, who I've never met].
Goal: [what you want them to do, e.g. fix the heating by Friday].
Background: [key facts, dates, what's happened so far].
Tone: [e.g. friendly but firm].

Constraints:
- Under 150 words.
- Put the request in the first two sentences.
- Make it easy to say yes: suggest a specific next step or time.
- No exaggeration and no guilt-tripping.

Give me a subject line and the email. Then give me one shorter alternative version.
✏️ Edit my writing without losing my voice

When you have a draft and want it clearer and tighter, but still sounding like you.

Please edit this text. It's a [type, e.g. cover letter / blog post / report section] for [audience].
My draft: [paste text].

- Keep my voice and my main points. Don't add new claims or facts.
- Fix grammar, cut unnecessary words, and break up long sentences.
- Flag (don't fix) anything that sounds unclear or unsupported.

Output:
1. The edited version.
2. A short list of the 5 most important changes and why you made them, so I can learn from them.
🎚️ Change the tone of a message

When a message sounds too blunt, too stiff, or too casual for the situation.

Rewrite this message so it sounds [target tone, e.g. warmer and less defensive / more professional / more confident].
Who will read it: [e.g. my manager / a customer who's upset / a group chat of parents].
Original: [paste message].

Keep the same meaning and length (within 20%). Give me 3 versions, from slightly adjusted to fully rewritten, and one sentence on how each one will likely come across.
🧠 Turn a messy brain-dump into a clear draft

When you have lots of ideas in no particular order and need a first draft.

I need to write a [type, e.g. proposal / speech / newsletter] for [audience]. Goal: [what it should achieve].
Here's my brain-dump of ideas, in no order: [paste notes].

Please:
1. First, suggest an outline (headings + one line each) and wait for my OK.
2. After I approve the outline, write the full draft at about [word count] words.
Use only my ideas. If something important seems missing, ask me rather than making it up.
🧐 Give me honest feedback on my writing

Before you send or submit something important and want a tough but fair second opinion.

Act as a demanding but kind reviewer. Here's my [essay / application / report] for [audience and purpose]: [paste text].

Give feedback in this order:
1. In one sentence: what the main point seems to be. (If it's not what I intended, that's my biggest problem.)
2. The 3 most important problems, most serious first, each with a concrete suggestion.
3. One thing that works well and should stay.
4. A score from 1–10 on clarity and on persuasiveness, with one reason each.
Don't rewrite it for me.
🕊️ Write a difficult message

When you need to say no, apologise, or give bad news and want to get it right.

I need to [say no / apologise / deliver bad news] to [who]. The situation: [what happened and what you need to say].
What I want to keep: [e.g. a good relationship with this client].

Write a message that is honest, kind, and clear. Avoid over-apologising and vague excuses. If there's a next step or alternative I can offer, include it.
Under 120 words. Then list 2 phrases I should avoid and why.
🔭 Write a deep-research brief

Before starting a deep-research run, so the agent searches for exactly what you need.

Please research this question for me: [your question].
Why I'm asking: [the decision or task this is for, e.g. choosing which electric car to lease].
What I already know: [anything you've already found or ruled out].

Requirements:
- Use recent, reputable sources and prefer primary ones (official sites, studies, original data).
- Cite every claim with a link. If sources disagree, show both sides.
- Mark anything uncertain or older than [e.g. 2 years].
- Don't include anything you couldn't find a source for.

Output: a 1-paragraph answer first, then key findings as bullets, then a table comparing [options or factors], then a list of sources.
⚖️ Compare my options side by side

When you're choosing between a few options (tools, courses, phones, providers) and feel overwhelmed.

Help me compare [option A], [option B] and [option C] for [what you'll use it for].
What matters most to me, in order: [e.g. price, ease of use, privacy].
My budget / limits: [e.g. under 30 a month, must work on Mac].

Make a comparison table with one row per factor and one column per option. Say "unknown" instead of guessing, and note which facts I should double-check on the official website because they change often.
Then give a recommendation for my situation in 3 sentences, and tell me what would change your recommendation.
🕵️ Check if this source is trustworthy

When you find an article, statistic or viral claim and aren't sure whether to believe it.

I found this claim: [paste the claim or text], from [where you found it, with link if you have one].

Help me evaluate it:
1. Who is behind the source, and what might their motive be?
2. Is there evidence, and is it original or second-hand?
3. Can you find other reputable sources that confirm or contradict it? Link them.
4. Any red flags (missing date, emotional language, numbers without a source)?
Finish with a verdict: likely true / unclear / likely misleading — and how confident you are.
📄 Summarise a long document for me

When you have a long report, article or PDF and need the key points quickly.

Summarise the attached document: [attach or paste it]. I'm reading it because [your purpose, e.g. I need to decide whether this policy affects my team].

Give me:
1. The main point in 2 sentences.
2. The 5 most important details for my purpose, each with the page or section number.
3. Anything surprising or that I might easily miss.
4. Questions the document leaves unanswered.
Only use what's in the document. Quote exact wording for any numbers or deadlines.
🚀 Get up to speed on a new topic fast

When you need to sound informed about a topic for a meeting, class or project — quickly.

I need to get up to speed on [topic] in about [time, e.g. 30 minutes] because [reason, e.g. I have a meeting with a supplier in this industry tomorrow].

Please give me:
1. A plain-language overview (under 200 words).
2. The 10 key terms I'll hear, each defined in one line.
3. The 3 big debates or open questions right now (as of today), with sources.
4. 5 smart questions I could ask an expert.
Point out anything that may have changed recently, and link sources for facts I might repeat.
🤺 Find the strongest arguments on both sides

When you're forming an opinion or preparing for a debate and want to avoid one-sided thinking.

Give me the strongest case for and against [position, e.g. a four-day work week for our 20-person company].

- 4 arguments for and 4 against, each with the best evidence you can find (with sources).
- Steelman each side: write it the way its smartest supporter would.
- Then list which facts the disagreement really hinges on.
Don't tell me what to think — but do tell me where the evidence is weak on either side.
📥 Triage my inbox

When your inbox is overflowing and you need to know what to deal with first.

Help me triage these emails. My role: [e.g. office manager]. My priorities this week: [e.g. the budget deadline on Thursday].
Emails: [paste subject lines and short snippets, or connect your email if your assistant can].

Sort them into:
- 🔴 Reply today (with one line on why)
- 🟡 This week
- 🔵 Just read / FYI
- ⚪ Can archive
For each 🔴 email, draft a 2–3 sentence reply for me to review. Don't send anything.
✅ Turn meeting notes into action items

Right after a meeting, to make sure everyone knows who's doing what.

Here are notes or a transcript from our meeting about [topic]: [paste].
Attendees: [names and roles].

Please produce:
1. A 3-sentence summary of what was decided.
2. An action-item table: Task | Owner | Deadline. If the owner or deadline wasn't said, write "not agreed" — don't guess.
3. Open questions that still need an answer.
4. A short, friendly follow-up email I can send to attendees.
📅 Plan my week

On Sunday evening or Monday morning, to turn a long to-do list into a realistic week.

Help me plan my week. Here's everything on my plate: [list tasks, with deadlines if any].
My fixed commitments: [meetings, classes, family time].
My energy: [e.g. I focus best in the morning; Wednesday afternoons are always busy].

- Pick my top 3 priorities for the week and explain why.
- Schedule deep-focus work in my best hours, and keep at least 20% of time free for surprises.
- Tell me what to drop, delegate or postpone if it doesn't fit — be honest.
Format: a day-by-day plan with time blocks, then the "not this week" list.
🧩 Break a big project into small steps

When a project feels too big to start.

I need to [project, e.g. organise a team offsite for 25 people] by [deadline]. It feels overwhelming.

Break it into steps:
- Group steps into 3–5 phases.
- Each step should take under 2 hours and start with a verb.
- Mark steps that depend on others, and the ones I could hand to someone else.
- Tell me the very first step I can do in the next 15 minutes.
Format: a checklist I can copy into my to-do app.
📋 Write a clear checklist for a recurring task

When you do the same task often (month-end, onboarding, travel packing) and keep forgetting steps.

I regularly [task, e.g. close the books at the end of each month / prepare for a client visit]. Here's roughly how I do it: [describe steps, in any order].

Turn this into a reliable checklist:
- In the right order, grouped into stages.
- Each item is one clear action with a "done" condition.
- Add any common steps I might be forgetting, marked "(suggested)".
- Note which steps are easy to get wrong and how to double-check them.
🗂️ Prepare me for a meeting

Before an important meeting, so you walk in with a clear goal and questions.

I have a meeting with [who] about [topic] on [date]. My goal for the meeting: [what a good outcome looks like].
Background: [relevant emails, notes or documents].

Give me:
1. A one-paragraph briefing on where things stand.
2. A suggested agenda (3–4 items, with minutes for each).
3. 5 questions I should ask, and 3 tough questions they might ask me, with short suggested answers.
4. The one thing I must not leave without.
📊 Write my weekly status report

At the end of the week, when you need to update your manager or team quickly.

Turn my notes into a weekly status report for [audience, e.g. my manager and the project sponsor].
My notes: [paste what you worked on, progress, problems].

Format:
- Overall status: 🟢 on track / 🟡 at risk / 🔴 off track, with one sentence why.
- Done this week (3–5 bullets, results not activities).
- Next week (3 bullets).
- Risks or blockers, and what help I need.
Under 200 words. Plain language, no jargon. Don't make progress sound better than my notes say.
🧹 Clean up a messy spreadsheet

When you have a spreadsheet with duplicates, inconsistent formats or blank cells.

I have a spreadsheet of [what it contains, e.g. 800 customer contacts] that's messy. I've attached it [or pasted a sample of 20 rows].
I'll use it for [purpose, e.g. a mailing list import].

Please:
1. First, list the problems you find (duplicates, inconsistent dates, typos in categories, empty cells) with counts. Don't change anything yet.
2. Propose cleaning rules and wait for my OK.
3. After I approve, apply them and give me the cleaned file plus a log of every change.
Never delete rows without listing them first.
🎤 Practise a job interview

In the days before a job interview, to rehearse answers and get feedback.

Act as an interviewer for a [job title] role at [type of company]. Here is the job description: [paste it]. My background: [short summary or paste CV].

- Ask me one question at a time, mixing behavioural ("Tell me about a time…") and role-specific questions.
- After each answer, give feedback: what was strong, what was missing, and how to use the STAR structure (Situation, Task, Action, Result) better.
- Include one tough or unexpected question.
After 6 questions, summarise my 2 biggest strengths and the 2 things to practise most.
🧭 Get up to speed in a new job

In your first weeks at a new job, to learn faster and ask the right questions.

I just started as a [job title] at a [type and size of organisation]. My manager expects me to [key responsibility] within [time frame].
What I've learned so far: [notes, org chart, documents].

Help me make a 30-60-90 day plan:
- What to learn, who to meet, and what early wins to aim for in each period.
- 10 good questions to ask my manager and colleagues in the first two weeks.
- A glossary of terms I've seen and don't fully understand: [list terms].
Keep it practical and realistic for someone who's still learning the ropes.
📃 Improve my CV for a specific job

When applying for a job and you want your CV to match what the employer is looking for.

Here's the job ad: [paste]. Here's my CV: [paste].

Please:
1. List the 5 most important requirements in the ad, and how well my CV shows each one (strong / weak / missing).
2. Suggest rewrites for up to 6 bullet points so they show results with numbers where I have them.
3. Tell me which experiences to move up or cut.
Don't invent experience, numbers or skills I haven't listed. If a number would help, ask me for it.
🗣️ Prepare for a tough conversation at work

Before asking for a raise, giving critical feedback, or pushing back on a decision.

I need to [have the conversation, e.g. ask my manager for a raise / tell a colleague their deadlines are slipping].
Context: [relationship, history, facts].
What I want to happen: [outcome].

Help me prepare:
1. A short opening (2–3 sentences) I can actually say out loud.
2. My 3 key points, backed by facts.
3. The 3 most likely reactions and how I could respond calmly to each.
4. What I'm willing to accept as a compromise.
Then role-play the other person so I can practise. Push back realistically.
🌉 Explain a work topic to a non-expert

When you need to explain something technical to a client, boss or another team.

I need to explain [topic, e.g. why the website launch is delayed by two weeks] to [audience, e.g. our sales team, who aren't technical].
The facts: [paste details].

Write an explanation that:
- Starts with what it means for them, not with the technical details.
- Uses one everyday analogy.
- Is under 150 words, with no jargon.
- Ends with what they need to do (or that they don't need to do anything).
Then give me 3 questions they're likely to ask, with short answers.
🧳 Plan a trip that fits my style

When you're planning a holiday and want an itinerary that suits your budget and pace.

Help me plan a [number]-day trip to [destination] in [month]. Travellers: [who, e.g. two adults and a 7-year-old].
Budget: [amount, and what it includes]. Style: [e.g. relaxed, love food markets, hate long drives].
Must-dos: [anything fixed]. Avoid: [anything you dislike].

- Give a day-by-day plan with a morning, afternoon and evening idea, grouped by area to limit travel.
- Include one rainy-day backup per day.
- Estimate costs, and mark opening hours, prices and bookings I should double-check, since they change.
Format: one short section per day, then a packing checklist.
💰 Make a simple monthly budget

When you want to understand where your money goes and set a realistic budget.

Help me build a simple monthly budget. My take-home income: [amount]. My fixed costs: [rent, bills, subscriptions].
Roughly what I spend on other things: [groceries, transport, eating out, etc.]. My goal: [e.g. save 200 a month for a holiday].

- Put it in a table: Category | Current | Suggested | Notes.
- Suggest 3 realistic changes that get me to my goal, starting with the least painful.
- Point out any spending that looks unusually high compared with the rest.
I don't want investment or tax advice — just a clear, practical plan.
🤔 Help me make a decision

When you're stuck between choices (a move, a job offer, a big purchase) and going round in circles.

I'm trying to decide between [option A] and [option B]. Background: [your situation].
What matters to me: [values and priorities].
My worries: [what's holding you back].

Help me think it through — don't decide for me:
1. Ask me up to 3 clarifying questions first.
2. Then make a weighted pros-and-cons table based on what matters to me.
3. Ask: "Which option would you regret not choosing in 5 years?"
4. Suggest a small, cheap way I could test one option before committing.
🥗 Plan meals for the week

When you want to eat better, waste less food and spend less time deciding what's for dinner.

Plan [number] dinners for this week for [who, e.g. 2 adults and a picky 9-year-old].
Dietary needs: [allergies, vegetarian, etc.]. Time per meal: [e.g. under 30 minutes on weekdays].
Budget: [amount]. Already in my fridge: [ingredients to use up].

- Reuse ingredients across meals to reduce waste.
- Mark which meals can be cooked ahead or frozen.
- Give me one combined shopping list, grouped by supermarket section.
Format: a table (Day | Meal | Time | Notes), then the shopping list.
📜 Understand a confusing letter or contract

When you get an official letter, bill or contract full of jargon and don't know what it means for you.

Please explain this [letter / contract / bill] in plain language: [paste text — remove account numbers and personal details first].

Tell me:
1. What it's about, in 2 sentences.
2. What I need to do, and by when.
3. Any costs, penalties or commitments, quoted exactly.
4. Anything unusual that I should ask about.
Remind me which points I should confirm with a qualified professional or the sender, since you're not giving legal or financial advice.
🎉 Plan a celebration

When you're organising a birthday, party or family gathering and want it to feel easy.

Help me plan [event, e.g. my mum's 60th birthday lunch] for about [number] people on [date].
Budget: [amount]. Venue: [home / restaurant / undecided]. The guest of honour loves [interests].

Give me:
- 3 theme or format ideas, each in one sentence.
- A countdown checklist (4 weeks, 2 weeks, 1 week, the day before, on the day).
- A simple timeline for the day itself.
- 2 small personal touches that would make it memorable.
🤝 Delegate a multi-step task to an agent

When you're handing an agent a task with several steps and want it done right the first time.

Task: [what you want done, e.g. research 5 venues for our team dinner and draft an invitation].
Why: [the purpose, so you can make good judgment calls, e.g. we want people to mix across teams].
Context: [constraints, preferences, files or links to use].

How to work:
- Start by restating the goal and listing your planned steps.
- Use only the sources and tools I've mentioned unless you ask first.
- If you hit a dead end, try one alternative, then tell me what happened.

Done means: [concrete finish line, e.g. a table of 5 venues with price, capacity and link, plus a draft invitation under 100 words]. Tell me anything you weren't sure about.
🛑 Plan first, then ask before risky actions

When an agent will take actions in the real world (emails, files, purchases, code) and you want to stay in control.

I'd like your help with: [task].

Before you do anything:
1. Write a step-by-step plan and wait for my approval.
2. In the plan, mark every step that sends something, deletes something, spends money, or can't easily be undone.

While working:
- Ask me before each marked step, showing exactly what you're about to do.
- Never delete or overwrite files — make copies instead.
- If something unexpected happens, stop and tell me rather than improvising.

At the end, give me a short log of every action you took.
🔍 Review an agent's work

After an agent finishes a report, analysis or piece of work, before you rely on it.

Please review the work you just produced (or this work: [paste]) as a sceptical expert would.

1. List every factual claim, number and citation. For each one, say whether you verified it against a source and how.
2. Point out any step where you made an assumption or guessed.
3. Check the maths and recalculate any totals or percentages.
4. Name the 3 weakest parts and how to make them stronger.
Be honest — I'd rather know about problems now than find them later.
🎙️ Ask the agent to interview me first

When you're not sure how to describe what you want, and you'd like the agent to draw it out of you.

I want help with [rough idea, e.g. a plan to get fit / a website for my bakery], but I haven't worked out the details.

Before you produce anything, interview me:
- Ask one question at a time, up to 8 questions.
- Focus on my goal, constraints, preferences, and what a great result would look like.
- After the questions, summarise what you understood in 5 bullets and ask me to confirm.
Only then create [the deliverable].
⚙️ Set up a reusable instruction for an agent

When you keep giving an assistant the same background and want to write it once (custom instructions, project instructions or a skill).

Help me write standing instructions for an AI assistant that will help me with [recurring work, e.g. writing weekly newsletters for our gardening club].

Ask me about: my role, the audience, the tone I like, things to always do, things to never do, and the usual format of the output.
Then write the instructions:
- Under 250 words, in plain bullet points.
- Include one short example of a good output.
- Include a rule to ask me when information is missing instead of making it up.
🪞 Have an agent check its own work before handing it over

When accuracy matters and you want the agent to catch its own mistakes before you see the result.

Task: [what you want, e.g. summarise these 3 reports into a one-page comparison].

When you've finished a draft, don't give it to me yet. First:
1. Re-read my request and check that every part is answered.
2. Verify every number and quote against the original material.
3. Remove anything you can't support.
Then give me the final version, followed by a short "What I checked" list and anything you're still unsure about.
👥 Split a big job between a team of agents

When you're using an agent that can run helpers in parallel and the job naturally splits into parts.

Goal: [big task, e.g. compare 6 project-management tools for a 15-person non-profit].

Please plan this as a team effort:
1. Split the work into independent parts, one per helper (e.g. one tool each), with clear instructions for each.
2. Give every helper the same output format: [e.g. price, key features, limits, links to sources].
3. When they're done, combine the results, check for contradictions, and resolve or flag them.
Show me the split before you start, and tell me roughly how much longer or more expensive this will be than doing it alone.
